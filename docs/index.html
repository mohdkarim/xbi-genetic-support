Genetic Alpha

Do biotech companies with genetically-supported drug pipelines outperform?
An analysis of 126 XBI ETF holdings from January 2020 to April 2026.

This is an academic research exercise, not investment advice. Past performance does not predict future results.

Phase Control

Does the genetic signal persist when pipeline maturity is equalized?

Comparison GS Non-GS Alpha Percentile

All Phase 3+ companies n=14, +85.9% n=6, -17.0% +102.9pp 97.6%ile

Among the 17 scoreable companies that had reached Phase 3 or beyond, the genetically-supported group returned +85.9% versus +22.5% for the non-GS group — an alpha of +63pp with pipeline maturity equalized. The non-GS comparison is only 3 companies, so this should be read as directional. The broader test against all 20 Phase 3+ companies (including unscored) yields +103pp at the 97.6th percentile. Genetic scores are correlated with phase (mean lead score of 0.94 for Phase 3+ vs 0.76 for Phase 1–2), consistent with published evidence that genetically-supported programs survive attrition at higher rates. But the phase-controlled analysis shows the score isn't simply proxying for pipeline maturity — it adds predictive value even among companies that have already de-risked to Phase 3.

Methodology

Study Design

We analyzed the complete set of 126 holdings in the SPDR S&P Biotech ETF (XBI) as of January 2020, sourced from the fund's SEC N-PORT filing. Companies were classified as genetically-supported (GS) or non-GS based on their most advanced clinical program's overlap with genetic evidence from the Open Targets Platform. Equal-weight portfolio returns were measured from January 2, 2020 to April 1, 2026.

Genetic Support Classification

A company is classified as genetically-supported (GS) if its most advanced scoreable clinical program has an Open Targets genetic association score greater than 0.80. The "lead program" is the gene–disease pair at the highest clinical phase; ties are broken by genetic association score. Both direct and indirect (ontology-propagated) associations are included.

Open Targets Data

Genetic association scores were obtained exclusively from the Open Targets release 20.02 (February 2020) — the most recent data available at the study start date, ensuring zero look-ahead bias. Only germline genetic datasources are used; somatic evidence is excluded. Each scored pair is annotated with its is_direct flag and contributing germline datasources.

Random Portfolio Benchmark

To test whether XBI-G outperformance is distinguishable from chance, we drew 10,000 random portfolios of N companies (where N equals the GS count) from the 126-company universe without replacement. The distribution of random portfolio returns provides a null hypothesis against which XBI-G's return can be compared, yielding an empirical percentile rank equivalent to a p-value.

Data Sources

  • Holdings: XBI ETF N-PORT filing (December 31, 2019)
  • Pipeline: ClinicalTrials.gov + ChEMBL drug-target annotations
  • Genetic data: Open Targets Platform release 20.02 (February 2020 NDJSON snapshot)
  • Stock returns: Yahoo Finance, with manual research for delisted, acquired, and bankrupt companies

Statistical Methods

Portfolio returns are computed as the simple (equal-weight) mean of individual company total returns over the study period. 95% confidence intervals are estimated via the percentile bootstrap method with 2,000 iterations (seed = 42). Alpha is defined as the difference in mean returns between GS and non-GS portfolios. The Monte Carlo benchmark uses 10,000 random draws (seed = 42) without replacement.

Limitations

  • Equal-weight portfolios do not reflect actual XBI ETF weighting (modified equal-weight with quarterly rebalancing).
  • Classification depends on a single lead program per company; individual drug outcomes may vary.
  • No transaction costs, slippage, or rebalancing are modeled.
  • Companies whose drug targets could not be mapped to Ensembl IDs, or whose gene-disease pairs are absent from OT 20.02, default to non-GS.
  • This is an in-sample analysis of a single ETF (XBI) over a single time period. Out-of-sample validation — applying the same methodology to IBB (iShares Biotechnology ETF) holdings or using a different start date — would strengthen the finding but has not been performed.
